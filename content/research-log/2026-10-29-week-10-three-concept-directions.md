## What I did
- Turned last week's research into three concept directions:
  - 01: 3D-printed lattice midsole (engineered cushioning)
  - 02: Achilles-focused running shoe built around heel-to-toe drop and heel geometry (biomechanical design)
  - 03: Modular arch-support sneaker with interchangeable inserts (personalized support)
- Wrote a central research question for each concept
- Mapped out how each one could be tested
- Read Carbon's write-up on advanced lattices

## What I found
- [Published] Carbon describes lattices as internal 3D structures that make a material's properties programmable, with density and shape able to vary within one part (Carbon, 2025)
- [Published] Carbon's design software sets lattice type, cell size, and strut diameter per zone of a part (Carbon, 2025)
- [Claim] Carbon states lattices can't be made with traditional processes like injection molding or machining (Carbon, 2025)

## Engineering decisions
- Concept 01 research question: how does lattice relative density affect compressive stiffness, deformation, and energy absorption? Testing: compression testing of printed samples
- Concept 02 research question: can shoe geometry influence Achilles tendon loading? Testing: gait analysis and ankle motion, with tendon load estimated through modeling, depending on available equipment
- Concept 03 research question: can interchangeable inserts adapt one sneaker to different support needs? Testing: plantar pressure mapping, fit, and comfort ratings

## Problems and failures
- Concept 02: tendon loading is hard to measure directly, so validating it may need modeling or specialized lab equipment
- Concept 03: removable insoles already exist, so I'd need to clearly define what's new
