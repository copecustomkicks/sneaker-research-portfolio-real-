## Objectives for the week

- Build a reverse tech pack for a real shoe (Nike Air Force 1 Low) as a structured documentation exercise, not a new design.
- Apply the final stretch of FASH 912 — manufacturing problem-solving and factory communication — to that exercise.

## Activities completed

- Selected the Nike Air Force 1 Low as the reverse-tech-pack subject, using an all-white, size 7Y reference pair. Reviewed it from lateral, medial, top, outsole, and detail angles.
- Oriented the documentation around the shoe's original 1983 basketball-performance context (era-appropriate competitors like the adidas Top Ten and Converse Pro Leather, period-relevant terminology and timelines) rather than a modern relaunch, and kept an earlier hypothetical "2028 relaunch" draft separate so the two scenarios' specs don't mix. Written in company-facing language, without personal attribution, to read like an internal document.
- Drafted a 13-part document set covering the categories a real tech pack needs: product brief, blank CAD, component callouts, construction-detail callouts, material CAD, BOM, shell pattern, tooling drawings, cost breakdown, workback calendar, wear-test plan, factory/development summary, and supporting layout.
- Finished the last week of FASH 912: manufacturing-constraint and feasibility evaluation, prototype-vs-production differences, factory revision communication, and a structured failure-diagnosis method (identify the defect, generate corrective options, justify the one selected).
- Attended two industry briefings (Oct 5, Oct 12) on Nike's product-development process — faster prototyping, athlete-test feedback loops, and how product requirements interact with manufacturing constraints.

## Key findings

- A finished shoe hides a large amount of interconnected decision-making — materials, pattern, tooling, assembly, and cost all constrain each other, which only became concrete by trying to document an existing shoe backward.
- Reconstructing an older product's specs requires explicitly separating confirmed historical fact from reasonable assumption — otherwise a reconstruction reads as more certain than it is.
- Documentation quality is itself a manufacturing variable: ambiguous specs are a direct cause of factory errors, not just a communication nicety.

## Engineering decisions

- Keep the Air Force 1 historical reconstruction and the earlier 2028-relaunch concept as two separate documents rather than merging their assumptions.
- Carry the tech-pack document structure (the 13 categories above) forward as the template for documenting my own eventual prototype.
- Label every reconstructed historical spec as an assumption, not a confirmed fact, since none of it has been checked against the physical reference shoe yet.
